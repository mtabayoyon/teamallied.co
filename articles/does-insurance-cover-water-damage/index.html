Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage?

It depends on the cause. Here's a plain-English breakdown of what's typically covered, what isn't, and how good documentation protects you.

Sudden and accidental is usually covered

Most homeowners policies cover water damage that's sudden and accidental — a burst pipe, a failed water heater, an overflowing appliance. The key words insurers use are "sudden" and "accidental."

Gradual damage and neglect usually aren't

Damage from a long-term slow leak, lack of maintenance, or wear and tear is commonly denied. This is exactly why fast response matters — addressing a loss quickly keeps it in "sudden" territory.

Flooding from outside is separate

Water that rises from outside — storm surge, overflowing creeks, groundwater — is generally not covered by standard homeowners insurance and requires separate flood insurance.

Documentation is your protection

Thorough photos, moisture readings, and a clear scope of the damage protect you if the claim is questioned. A restoration company that documents properly is doing more than cleanup — it's building your case.

We bill insurance directly

Allied documents losses in the format insurers expect and can bill directly in most cases, which takes a major burden off you during an already stressful time. This article is general information, not insurance advice — always confirm specifics with your own carrier and policy.
